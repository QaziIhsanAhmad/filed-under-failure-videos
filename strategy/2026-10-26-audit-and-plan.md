### Bottlenecks the evidence supports
1. **Long-form gets almost no distribution.** 47 views across 4 episodes. Watch hours — the slowest YPP requirement — are close to zero. Shorts are not converting into episode views (2,700 Short views → 47 episode views).
2. **Topic demand.** Three of four episodes are UK stories with weak current search demand (strategy/2026-10-08: "carillion collapse" ≈1 view/day for recent videos). Theranos news peg did not beat SVB Shorts (median 39 vs 27; under 2x = inconclusive).
3. **Packaging.** Every episode uses "Why Did X Collapse? …" or similar. With no CTR data this can't be proven, but these titles got 2–29 views. The best Short framed human stakes ("Who paid…"), not a statistic.
4. **Length.** 13–16 min episodes from a 1-subscriber channel; target is now 8–12 min.
5. **Monetisation-review risk.** All videos share one format: synthetic narrator + code-generated text cards. Scripts are original and sourced, but YouTube's inauthentic-content policy (in force since 15 Jul 2025) names "slideshows that all have the same narration." Mitigation: vary visuals (licensed photos/maps/documents), keep original analysis prominent, avoid near-identical Shorts.
6. **Measurement.** No CTR, retention, traffic source or Earn counter data is reaching automation. Every decision below is therefore based on public views only.
7. **Reporting cadence bug.** The "weekly growth review" task was running monthly (1st of month) since 8 Oct. Fixed today → Mondays 09:41 PKT.

## 6. Episode 006 — production package

**Central question:** How does a company with ~$5bn of annual sales end up with $12m in cash and $9bn+ of liabilities — and where did the money go?

**Why this topic first:** US/global audience (higher-value market, larger search pool), live news (Chapter 7 conversion Aug 2026, brothers' blame filings Sept–Oct 2026, trial Feb 2027), household brands (Fram, Autolite), and a clean explanatory hook (invoice finance). Built-in follow-up episode when the trial ends → natural subscribe reason.

**Titles (pick 1, keep others for a later A/B):**
1. First Brands: $5 Billion in Sales, $12 Million in Cash *(recommended)*
2. Where Did First Brands' Money Go? The Car-Parts Collapse
3. The Fram and Autolite Owner That Went Bust Owing $9 Billion

**Thumbnails:**
- A: "$12M CASH" huge red over "$9B DEBT" white; generic spark-plug/oil-filter photo (Pexels/Pixabay, no logos) cracked down the middle.
- B: "SOLD TWICE?" over a drawn invoice stamped twice in red (question mark keeps it an allegation).

**Opening (stakes in ~10 s):** "A car-parts company selling five billion dollars a year went bankrupt with twelve million dollars in the bank. And more than nine billion dollars of debts."

**Facts vs allegations:** Verified = filing date, cash/liabilities, Chapter 7 conversion, guilty pleas (Brumbergs, Graham), trial date, lenders' statements. Allegations = fake/inflated invoices, double pledging, round trips, hidden inventory financing, personal enrichment (all denied; presumed innocent). Analysis = the three lessons.

**Visual plan:** pipeline cards (stat, bars, timeline, document, quote) + 3–5 licensed real images to break the template (car-parts aisle, Manhattan federal courthouse exterior, Cleveland skyline — Wikimedia Commons/Pexels, credited). Invoice-flow diagram for factoring and "round trips". No photos of the defendants unless properly licensed.

**Closing lesson:** compare cash with sales; debt you can't see is still debt; when each lender sees only its own slice, nobody sees the whole.

**Subscribe / next video:** "The trial is set for February. Subscribe if you want the follow-up when a jury decides." End screen → 005 Patisserie Valerie (hidden overdrafts), then Greensill (008) when live.

**Length:** 57 scenes, ~1,110 words ≈ 9 min.

**Built on borrowed money**

- Chapter 1. Built on borrowed money.  
  _Visual: chapter — Built on borrowed money_
- Patrick James founded the business in Ohio in 2013. It was then called Crowne Group.  
  _Visual: timeline — Crowne Group founded in Ohio by Patrick James_ · _Source: Truck Parts & Service, Mar 2026; AP, 29 Jan 2026_
- It grew by buying parts makers, wiper brand Trico among them, mostly with borrowed money. In August 2020 the group renamed itself First Brands.  
  _Visual: timeline — Trico Group renamed First Brands Group_ · _Source: CAT Magazine, 18 Aug 2020_
- From the outside, it looked boring in a good way. Cars always need filters, wipers and brake pads.

**Selling tomorrow's money today**

- Chapter 2. Selling tomorrow's money today.  
  _Visual: chapter — Selling tomorrow's money today_
- When a parts maker ships to a retailer, the retailer often pays months later. That unpaid bill is called a receivable.  
  _Visual: document — Receivable_ · _Source: Filed Under Failure analysis_
- Instead of waiting, the company can sell the bill to a finance firm, called a factor, for a bit less than it's worth. The factor collects the full amount later.  
  _Visual: list — How factoring works_ · _Source: Filed Under Failure analysis_
- This is legal and very common. A related version, used to pay suppliers early, is called supply-chain finance.  
  _Visual: quote — Factoring and supply-chain finance are normal, legal tools_ · _Source: Filed Under Failure analysis_
- It only works if two things are true. The invoice is real. And it's sold only once.  
  _Visual: stats — Factoring only works if_ · _Source: Filed Under Failure analysis_

  _Visual: title — Two rules_

**One question too many**

- Chapter 3. The deal that asked one question too many.  
  _Visual: chapter — One question too many_

  _Visual: stat — $5.6bn_ · _Source: OHA (T. Rowe Price), Oct 2025_

  _Visual: quote — increasingly risky and expensive working capital financing_ · _Source: OHA (T. Rowe Price), Oct 2025_

  _Visual: stat — -83%_ · _Source: OHA (T. Rowe Price), Oct 2025_
- OHA said some of the off-balance-sheet financing carried interest rates above thirty per cent.  
  _Visual: stat — 30%+_ · _Source: OHA (T. Rowe Price), Oct 2025_
- On the twenty-eighth of September 2025, First Brands filed for bankruptcy in Texas.  
  _Visual: timeline — Chapter 11 filing, Southern District of Texas_ · _Source: US Attorney's Office SDNY, 29 Jan 2026_
- It reported about five billion dollars in annual sales, twelve million dollars in cash, and more than nine billion dollars in liabilities.  
  _Visual: stats — At the filing_ · _Source: US Attorney's Office SDNY, 29 Jan 2026_

  _Visual: chapter — Up to $2.3 billion_

  _Visual: timeline — Raistone asks for an independent examiner_ · _Source: Vehicle Service Pros, 20 Oct 2025_

  _Visual: quote — up to $2.3 billion in assets unaccounted for_ · _Source: Vehicle Service Pros, 20 Oct 2025_

  _Visual: stat — $1.9bn_ · _Source: Vehicle Service Pros, 20 Oct 2025_

**The indictment**

- Chapter 5. The indictment.  
  _Visual: chapter — The indictment_
- On the twenty-ninth of January 2026, federal prosecutors in New York charged Patrick James and his brother Edward, a former senior executive. Both were arrested in Ohio that day.  
  _Visual: timeline — Patrick and Edward James charged and arrested_ · _Source: US Attorney's Office SDNY, 29 Jan 2026_
- These are accusations. Both men pleaded not guilty and are presumed innocent.  
  _Visual: quote — merely accusations, and the defendants are presumed innocent_ · _Source: US Attorney's Office SDNY, 29 Jan 2026_
- Prosecutors allege that from at least 2018, the company used fake and inflated invoices to raise money from factors, including on bills that didn't exist.  
  _Visual: document — Fake and inflated invoices_ · _Source: US Attorney's Office SDNY, 29 Jan 2026_

  _Visual: bars — One invoice, as alleged_ · _Source: Truck Parts & Service, Mar 2026_

  _Visual: bars — Three days later, as alleged_ · _Source: Truck Parts & Service, Mar 2026_

  _Visual: stat — 2-3x_ · _Source: US Attorney's Office SDNY, 29 Jan 2026_

  _Visual: quote — corporate initiatives_ · _Source: US Attorney's Office SDNY, 29 Jan 2026_

  _Visual: document — Hidden inventory financing_ · _Source: US Attorney's Office SDNY, 29 Jan 2026_
- In July 2025, according to the indictment as reported, a senior executive told a lender there was no off-balance-sheet financing. Prosecutors put it at about two billion dollars.  
  _Visual: bars — Off-balance-sheet financing, July 2025_ · _Source: Truck Parts & Service, Mar 2026_

## Shorts (one fact each, linked to the episode)


**a. $5 billion in sales. $12 million in the bank. The First Brands collapse #Shorts**

- This company sold about five billion dollars of car parts a year. When it went bankrupt, it had twelve million dollars in the bank.
- Its debts? More than nine billion dollars.
- It was First Brands, the owner of Fram oil filters and Autolite spark plugs. It filed for bankruptcy in Texas in September 2025.
- Prosecutors say fake invoices and hidden debts were behind it. The founder and his brother deny it, and go on trial in February 2027.
- The full story is on our channel.

**b. One invoice, sold for $17,826 and then $463,735 – the First Brands allegation #Shorts**

- Prosecutors say one invoice for eight thousand nine hundred and seventy-six dollars was sold to a finance firm for almost eighteen thousand dollars.
- Three days later, they say, the same invoice was sold to a second firm for nearly four hundred and sixty-four thousand dollars.
- Invoice finance only works if the bill is real and sold once. Each lender saw only its own copy.
- This is an allegation in the First Brands case. Founder Patrick James and his brother deny the charges; their trial is set for February 2027.
- The full story is on our channel.

**c. $1.9 billion collected. How much was in the account? "$0" #Shorts**

- After First Brands went bankrupt, a creditor told the court the company had collected about one point nine billion dollars on invoices other investors had already bought.
- That money should have been passed on. So how much was in the accounts meant to hold it? A company lawyer answered: zero dollars.
- The creditor, Raistone, said up to two point three billion dollars in assets were unaccounted for, and asked for an independent examiner.
- About four months later, the founder and his brother were charged with fraud. They deny it.
- The full story is on our channel.

**d. Two brothers, one fraud case – and each blames the other #Shorts**
