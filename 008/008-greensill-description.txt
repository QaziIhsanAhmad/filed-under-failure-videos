Greensill Capital collapse explained from the regulator, parliamentary and Insolvency Service records.

In November 2020, $440m meant to repay a Credit Suisse fund arrived at Greensill Capital and, the Insolvency Service says, was used for other purposes. Four months later the firm was in administration and $10bn of fund money was frozen. Using FINMA's findings on Credit Suisse, the Treasury Committee and National Audit Office reports, BaFin's order, Lex Greensill's own evidence to MPs, Australian court documents and the 2026 disqualification undertaking, we explain how supply-chain finance turned into future claims on weak borrowers, why one insurance letter mattered so much, and who paid. A disqualification undertaking is not a criminal conviction; the SFO's GFG Alliance investigation is not a finding of guilt.
