Thomas Cook's collapse explained from the National Audit Office report: who paid to fly 140,000 stranded holidaymakers home, and why the government funded the rescue but refused the bailout.

On 23 September 2019 one of the world's oldest travel firms went into compulsory liquidation. Using the NAO's March 2020 investigation, the Commons business committee's questioning of chief executive Peter Fankhauser, Hansard, the company's own stock-exchange announcements, the CAA's annual report and the FRC's 2025 sanctions against auditor EY, we follow the debt, the final weekend, Operation Matterhorn and the estimated £83 million bill that fell on the taxpayer. Quotes are those of the people and bodies cited; 'Our analysis' is labelled as our own reading.
